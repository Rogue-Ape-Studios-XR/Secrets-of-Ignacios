# Quest 2 hand-tracking quality test

## Personal tests
These tests are done by personally testing a few hand-tracking applications on the quest 2.

## Environment and test procedure
A well it room filled with natural light and a quest 2 headset. The two applications tested where quest 2’s own menus with hand-tracking and the spellcasting game called Drakheir.

## Menu’s
The biggest complains with the menu’s is HOW the quest has implemented hand tracking. As you need to hold your hands in front of you, which makes a cursor appear on the menus in front of you, then when pinching you can ‘click’ whatever the cursor is hovering over. It feels weird as it basically feels like you’re imitating a crab.
But I never had any problem with hand recognition, it almost always accurately showed where my hands where and recognized my pinches.

## Drakheir
Drakheir is a combat-based spellcasting game that uses hand-tracking to allow the player to cast spells. Most spells worked, but the entire experience was a bit lacking. The biggest problem I personally encountered was the fact that a hand gesture that included holding your hands together as if clapping never really worked properly.
Beyond that, most gestures took at most 3 tries to get right, whether or not that this is Drakheir or quest 2’s fault is unclear however.

## Conclusion
In my experience Quest 2’s hand-tracking is working, but not always 100 percent reliable. This shouldn’t be a problem for Secrets of Ignacios, as there are rarely any really time sensitive moments like in Drakheir where whether or not a spell works can make the difference between winning or losing a combat encounter.
If I were to further test this I should get a quest 3 or quest pro, write a more in-depth test and compare the two. Or get others to try out quest 2’s hand-tracking and see how they like it, since I am relatively patient when it comes to these things.
