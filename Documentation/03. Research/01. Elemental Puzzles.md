## Introduction

This analysis helps understanding how these mechanics can contribute to engaging gameplay and puzzles. They offer unique challenges that require logical thinking and creative thinking.  
This analysis will have a general focus on Breath of the Wild, but will draw comparison to the other games that utilize elemental puzzles.

## Elemental systems

In BOTW, the primary elements available are fire, water, ice and electricity.

- Fire is able to burn flammable objects, melting ice and if used on grass, it can create updrafts for the player’s glider.  
- Water isn't used as much as the other elements to directly do things, but is often a medium for cryonis. Divine Beast Vah Ruta[^1], has several different water puzzles where you have to manipulate the flow of water to stop and start various water wheels.  
- Ice is primarily used to freeze water via the cryonis ability. It can stop the flow of water, create platforms to stand on and can be melted by fire.  
- Electricity is used to power some devices. A good example of this is the Electric Path trial in the Dak Tah Shrine[^2]. In this shrine you have to use a cube that is fueled by electricity to power various objects to get to the end.

Aside from the applications in puzzles, the elements can also affect enemies, for example, setting them on fire, or electrocuting them, making them drop their weapon.

## Puzzle Designs 

BOTW has various ways it structures puzzles. Often including activating certain mechanisms with a variation of elements. A common one is using cryonis to create pathways with ice. The game often combines elements as well, such as having to create ice, then melt it &, like in the Kuh Takkar Shrine[^3].

While the puzzles have various elemental properties, so does the overworld itself. for example, Fire will be less effective when it's raining, and even be doused and electricity is a lot more effective during rain and in bodies of water.

## Player agency in puzzles. 

BOTW has a very free puzzle solving experience, TOTK even more. The puzzles have an intended solution, but the player can solve various puzzles in a multitude of unintentional ways. Some puzzles even have an intended solution, that feels like the player was finding an unintentional way to solve it.   
Due to the various ways the puzzles can be solved, the player is incentivized to try and experiment more, especially with the abilities and elements.  
The game doesn’t really have a failure state in puzzles either. All puzzles will eventually reset, or don’t even need to be reset. This gives the player enough time and freedom to do what they want without being punished for it.

##  Puzzle engagement 

The elemental puzzles are integrated into all other actions and make a lot of sense in the world. It doesn’t feel out of place, it just feels like it’s supposed to be there. The elemental puzzles in BOTW are optionally found, usually either in the overworld or in shrines, mainly shrines, they do usually lead to a worthwhile reward, luring the player into doing them.

## Other games that have elemental puzzles 

### Tomb Raider 2013 

For example in the Tomb of the Lost Adventurer[^4], you often have to use fire in order to progress. You use fire to burn barrels, use it to burn cloth, e.t.c. It primarily makes use of fire for burning various objects.  
in the tomb Hall of Ascension[^5], you have to utilize the various wind currents to progress. A notable example is when you have to utilize the wind currents in order to push a platform while you’re on it.  
In the tomb Flooded Vault[^6], you have to deal with electricity.There are bodies of water that are electrified, making it dangerous to traverse. The non electrified water is also harder to traverse, severely slowing the player down. This could lead to having to use an alternate movement option to get across.

### The Trine Series 

In Trine 2, there is a level called “Mosslight Marsh”[^7] In this level you are required to use the water to route it to several plants to make them grow and progress. This is another way water can be used, although not applicable to ours, as we are in a dungeon.  
In Trine 4 Snow Topped Heights[^8]. There are various ice based puzzles. One of these puzzles consists of changing the flowing water into various ice platforms so you can traverse these to continue.  
Another puzzle in the same level consists of having to use fireballs to destroy ice. Either melting or straight up destroying them.  
This level also features some wind puzzles. There are some places you cannot pass, as the wind is pushing you back. You can then block the flow of wind with an object, in order to be able to pass.

## Conclusion 

Elemental puzzles are present in a lot of games, whether or not it’s the main gimmick, or just part of the existing mechanics.  
Key elements:

- Elements like the basic fire, water, ice and electricity can be used in numerous ways. They can even be combined to create even more possible puzzles  
- Integrating these elements into the lore of the game (which we have) is also very important. This makes it so that these puzzles won’t feel weird or out of place, making them feel more natural.  
- The best elemental puzzles make sure that there is either 1 singular way to solve it, or make the player have several options, incentivizing creativity.  
- Making the elements interact with the environment can also make things more believable, as well as open new ways for puzzles to be made and solved.  
- Combining elements can be very good, but if used in the wrong way, this can leave the player confused. By having some basic combinations that are obvious, like water and fire for example, would be better than having a weird combination where 2 elements react to each other, that normally wouldn't. This still is an option, it just needs to be conveyed properly to the player, while making sense too.  
- Puzzles often have reward systems attached to them. In our game this will be just advancing to the next puzzle and unlocking more, but maybe there can be more attached to it.
