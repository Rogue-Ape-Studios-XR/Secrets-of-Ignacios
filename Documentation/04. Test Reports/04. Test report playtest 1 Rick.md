## Conclusion
- People were overall happy about the game.
- Talking about the evaluation criteria, the players were able to understand what the visual
effects meant (the magic circle).
- The usability of spellcasting could be improved in 3 points:
  - The initial learning curve of the hand signs.
  It is difficult to explain to the players what handsigns to use, most of the subtleties get
  lost. We could lean into the idea to make holograms of the hand signs appear when
  you learn a new spell, allowing players to see the exact finger positions and hand
  rotations. If this is clear, the players can cast spells fairly easily.
  - Aiming the spell you cast.
  Right now you have little idea where you are aiming and the spells trigger as soon as
  the gesture is recognized. These two factors make it so it’s very hard to hit the spot
  you want to hit. A slight delay between recognizing the gesture and casting the spell
  may help, as well as an indicator for where you are aiming.  
  - Player feedback on miscasting a spell.
  Right now if you do the incorrect hand sign after a start hand sign, the spell routine
  will persist and it will still expect another sign. This confused the players as there was
  no indication a spell with that sign first didn’t exist.

And lastly, the players had an easy time solving the fire puzzles. This is good as this was the
tutorial level- however, we should take into account that later puzzles could be more difficult.
They had a good idea on what puzzles to expect in the future related to the fire element.

Additionally, the following was found:
- The teleport was hard to control as it would repeat easily.
- The alternative movement causes a bit of motion sickness, likely due to the ball being
attached to the face.
- The gap in the wall has no collision even for the wall around it, so players solved the
vine puzzle without knowing it
- It’s very easy to walk through walls.
- Players did not find the gloomstalk, or did not think to burn it. It may be useful to put
this lower.
